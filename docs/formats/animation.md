# Animation: Borg6 (roadmap 1B)

`Borg6Animation` (`AidynRenewed.Rom.Graphics`) reads a Borg6, and `Borg6Player` plays it.

- **Borg6 (1,988 animations)** animate the transforms of Borg5 scenes. The file is a 0x10-byte header (the scene, the target count and offset, and the length in ticks), then 0x18-byte targets, 0x10-byte tracks, 8-byte keys and their floats, at offsets from the start of the data. Files are padded to 8 bytes.
- **A target is one transform,** with a 9-bit mask of the channels it drives: rotation XYZ, then position XYZ, then scale XYZ, one track per set bit. Most drive a model node. A few drive the camera or turn a light, all in cinematics. No animation drives a transform twice.
- **Tracks are polynomials stepped by forward differences.** A track's state is a value and its first three differences. Each 60 Hz tick adds each difference to the one before, as far as the current key's mask reaches: mask 1 holds, 2 and 3 step linearly, 6 and 7 quadratically, 14 and 15 cubically. The first key is always at tick 1 and sets a value (mask 15, 3, 1 or 7). Later keys mostly load only the differences (mask 14 or 2), so the curve carries on from its value; a few (mask 15) jump. Each key list ends with a marker at the animation's length, which the game never loads.
- **Timing** (`Borg6Player`, from `anim.cpp`): a track's counter starts at 1. On a tick where it equals the next key's tick, that key loads, then the state steps and the counter goes up. When the counter reaches the length, the track reloads its first key instead, without stepping. So the scene clock `aniTime` starts at 1, and at time *t* each value is its curve at *t*. The animation loops every `Length` ticks, which `AnimationTests` checks bit for bit for every Borg6.
- **Driven channels are replaced, not offset.** Each tick the game zeroes a driven channel, then adds the value times the animation's weight, which is always 1. Bit *k* of the mask writes the *k*th float from the transform's rotation (`+0x10`): rotation, then position, then scale, in the file's order (`Borg6Target.Clear` `0x80096de0`, `Borg6Target.ApplyTransform` `0x80096ca0`). The differences are also added into four vectors per transform that nothing reads.
- **What the game supports and the data never uses:** target types 0, 2 and 3 (fields of other Borgs, such as a Borg2 material's tint), playback modes other than looping, tracks shorter than their animation, and blending several animations by weight.
- **102 animations have no targets.** They hold their scene still for their length.
- **glTF export:** each Borg6 goes into its scene's `.glb` as a glTF animation named `borg6 <index>`, baked at one sample per tick, from time 1 to time `Length + 1`, which is time 1 again, so a clip lasts exactly one loop. Linear interpolation matches the game at every tick. Driven model nodes get translation, rotation and scale, because the pivot makes the translation depend on the rotation. Billboards below a driven node get their world position, the camera gets its look-at, and lights get their direction. The animations with no targets can't be glTF animations, so they're listed in the scene's `extras` as `stillAnimations`. A scene whose animations are all still has no glTF animation, so Godot gives it no animation player; the views' `ActorAnimator` runs its actor without one, since its states' lengths and the emitters its requests start are all it shows (13 actors, seven of them spells' and flasks' visuals, [combat](../systems/combat.md#traps)). Each animation's `extras` hold its Borg6 and `length`, which the core times actors' states by (`ActorClips`, [actors](actors.md)). The core reads the per-tick samples from the glTF itself rather than through Godot. Godot's glTF loader resamples every track at the rate `GenerateScene` is given, 30 a second by default, so the views pass a sample a tick (`GlbFile.BakeFps`, from `SceneNames.AnimationSamplesPerSecond`, which the exporter writes its times by): they pose a scene every tick, and at 30 every other tick fell between samples with the translation and rotation interpolated apart, which moves a node turning fast about a pivot away from its origin off the pivot ([cinematics](../systems/cinematics.md#drawing)). **Godot's loader also leaves out a track whose every key is the node's rest value,** so a node an animation holds at rest kept what the animation before left it, where the original writes every channel its Borg6 drives each tick (above). 143 of the 203 actors have a channel one state holds at rest and another moves. The worst was Phelan in the final cut-scene (`aidyn:actor/0x2918`): his lying states hold his hips turned down to the floor and his lost leg scaled to nothing at rest, so after his idle he stood on both legs, oddly posed, his death's pool of blood about his knee. The views pose those channels at rest from the glTF (`DrivenNodes`, read once a scene by `GlbScene.Driven`, and `TransformTracks`, after the player has posed the rest), in the runtime's actors and cinematics and the editor's model view. A transform a Borg6 doesn't drive at all keeps what the last animation left, as in the original.

The editor plays a scene's animations and an actor's states ([editor](../systems/editor.md)), and [rendering](../systems/rendering.md) has what's left of drawing them.
