# Scripts: the Borg13 VM (roadmap 2.4)

`AidynRenewed.Core.Scripts`. The format and what the importer writes are in [dialogue](../formats/dialogue.md).

`ScriptRunner` runs one script (`aidyn:dialogue/0x…`) against the flag network and the game state. It ports the walk over the nodes (`DialogNode.cpp`) and the callbacks that carry out the ops (`dialogue.cpp`, `dialogmode.cpp`), from the debug build's machine code. `GameState.RunScript` makes a runner against the game: its flags, its world stream of random numbers and the flag that rolls skill checks 0–21. Conversations, cut-scenes and the editor's script page all start theirs there; the editor passes a world of its own that fixes skill checks by hand.

## Its boundary

- **Rules** go to the flag network, and to `IScriptWorld` for the party, inventory, gold, skills, stats and experience ([characters](characters.md)). Where the debug build crashes on a failed call (taking an item the party doesn't have, adding to a full party, giving experience to a non-member), retail carries on, and so does the runner.
- **Presentation** goes out as cues on `IScriptHost` (ADR 0003): cameras, spawning, facing, walking and running, animations, held items, sounds and the fade. The runtime carries them out, and tests record them.
- **The player** answers the page: `Continue` moves on from a line, `Choose` takes a choice, and `Skip` ends the script, as the debug build's B does; 1.0 has no skip. While the scripted camera moves, the original drops input (see [cut-scenes](#cut-scenes)).
- **The player's name.** The runner replaces `{player}` (`ScriptRunner.PlayerToken`) in every line, choice and random line with the first party member's name as the page is made, so a rename ([ui](ui.md#renaming-alaron)) shows on the next page. With no one in the party the text keeps `{player}`.
- **Waits** end with `Resume` from the runtime: an actor arriving, an animation ending or the camera stopping. `Step(ticks)` counts down `Wait`, whose operand is in ticks.
- **Map objects remember** a skill check's result and a random line's roll (the original's `VoxelChart`, the port's `MapObjectMemory`: [saves](saves.md#map-objects-memory)), through `IScriptWorld.TryRecall` and `Remember`. So talking to someone again can't reroll a check or a rumour. Troubadour checks aren't remembered.
- **Randomness** comes from an `IRandom` stream (ADR 0006). `Lcg` is the original's generator, and `Range` is `Random::Range`.
- **Gating again.** The original gates a map's objects again by the flags only when it reloads the map (`loadGameBorgScenes`): after the pause menu, a shop, training, a battle, a cut-scene or a teleport, but not after a plain conversation or sign, whose changes show when the chunk next loads ([wanderers](wanderers.md#talking)). The port regates as the pause menu closes (`ChunkStreamer.Regate`) and rebuilds trek after a cut-scene, backdrop or help script (`Conversation.RebuildsTrek`).

- **Flags and the journal.** `SetFlag` and `ClearFlag` only write (`Dialogue.Effect`, `0x8004f7b8`, ops 0x20 and 0x21), and `CheckFlag` reads (`Dialogue.Condition`, `0x8004f680`, op 0x18), bringing the network up to date when its flag is derived. So a journal entry a script sets is told when something next reads a derived flag: a `CheckFlag` later in the script; trek's loading the chunks again after a cut-scene, backdrop or the help menu; or, after a conversation or sign, which stay in trek, whatever in trek reads one first ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)). Of the 15 entries conversations set, only Trahern's request (entry 4) is followed by one, and 4 more come as the conversation ends because a sound or tangible object where the talker stands reads one; the rest wait. The port tells every entry as trek next has nothing up, the user's choice ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)).

## How it walks

A node is **reached** when the walk arrives at it, and its setup ops run: the speaker from any op, then the settings (camera time, enemies, battle bytes, reset visited), then the cameras and actors. It's **left** when the player moves on from it, and leaving runs its first effect op. The condition and effect checks stop at the first one, as the data expects; ops 55, 59 and 60 aren't effects in the jump table (`ScriptRunner.CommandCheckJumpTable`, `0x800D5B08`). Where the walk can go on without the player, through an If or a line with no text, it does so at once. The condition ops, and the effect ops that change the game's state (the flags, gold, items, members and experience, with their sounds), are `ScriptOps`, which the plugins' triggers run too ([events](events.md)); the runner gives it the speaker, its kept skill checks and the map object that started it.

- **Lines** show their text. Leaving one goes to its link. With no link, it goes back to the last menu or case, which is reached again: its setup and effect run again, and it's shown with the choices not yet taken.
- **Ifs** take their first link when the condition is **exactly 1** (`bne v0, 1`, not nonzero), and their second otherwise. Every base-game condition in an If is 0 or 1, so this only matters for mods.
- **Cases** show their text and evaluate their condition. They count the links whose own range holds the value (each link's, not the case node's), taken or not. With two or more, the untaken matches are offered as choices. With fewer, the value is kept, and when the player moves on, the first untaken match is taken. The value is kept as 16 bits, and 65535 is the original's mark for none: a single-match case on exactly 65535 gold finds nothing and faults.
- **Menus** show their text and their untaken choices. Each choice is reached as it's added, so its setup ops run then, and a page's choices can have different speakers. Backdrops and the help menu offer taken choices again, and a backdrop's choice loop tests the next choice's slot after moving on, so it shows every choice.
- **Visited** is set when a node is left, not when it's reached. So a choice that ends the script is never marked, and it stays on offer.
- **Effects that end the script** (end, shop, battle, train, camp heal) end it at once. A cut-scene, backdrop or the help menu sets its done flag however it ends, including by `Skip` or a fault.

A page is the line and the choices under it, as the original's `diags` list holds them, with room for 9. The line loses its leading spaces, as the original trims them, but choices keep theirs. When the first choice has the same speaker as the line, a conversation shows the line alone first and then the choices under Alaron's portrait. What A does on a page is the core's `DialogueBox.Accept`, which every mode shares: it brings up the choices behind a line shown alone, else takes the cursor's choice or moves the script on.

## Ops that need more than a flag

- **`RandomLine`** rolls 0–100, which the map object remembers, and picks a node of the other script by `roll / 100 / (1 / count)` in single precision, where the count includes its blank nodes. A blank node shows node 0's text. Twelve rumour scripts end in a blank node, so they show their first rumour about a third of the time, twice as often as each of the others. The line shows under the node that asked for it, and moving on leaves that node. The other script's text doesn't go through the player-name and escape processing the main script gets at load, but no rumour uses either.
- **`CheckSkill`** asks for the party's skill check once per script (`Party::SkillCheck`, a roll against the party's best: see [characters](characters.md#the-party)), keeps it, and remembers it on the map object. The base game only checks Diplomat, Loremaster and Troubadour. While the state flags record's `randomSkillChecks` flag (3717, a debug option) is set, it rolls 0–21 instead, after the lookup.
- **`PlayAnimation`** waits for the animation to end in a cut-scene, which only animates actors it has spawned ([actors](#actors)). Nine conversation nodes use it too, all lines, on handovers and dismissals (in 890, `0x037B`, `0x0387`, `0x041B` and `0x046C`), mostly request 19, a give-or-take gesture. **In 1.0 they never play.** The line shows as any line does, its speaker talking for the press that brought it up; the effect, run as the line is left, looks the actor up from the page it has just cleared, finds no one, and waits for A with the same line showing again, so the line takes A twice. The user saw it at the lighthouse keeper's key (Borg13 890, `aidyn:dialogue/0x037A`, Overworld E15): "Here... take the key" with the keeper talking, the same line again with no one moving, then Alaron's "Thank you." the same way. Some of the nine couldn't play anyway: the Male Commoner's actor (`aidyn:actor/0x27C4`, the keeper's and Batrin's) has no state for 19, and `0x037B`'s node 26 sets no speaker.
  - **The port fixes it** ([ADR 0018](../adr/0018-the-port-fixes-bugs-and-sets-enhancements.md)), in conversations only, the user's choice: an animated line plays its request in place of the talk as it shows, and shows once. `ScriptRunner.Show` cues `AnimateCue(speaker, request)` as such a line shows, for the speaker the box shows, so a node with none of its own (`0x037B`'s 26, "Get out of my sight, worm!") takes the one before it (the Marquis, from node 25); the effect then does nothing, and nothing waits.
  - In trek, `Conversation` asks the speaker's body for the request in place of the talk for that press, by the talk's own rule ([wanderers](wanderers.md#talking)), if the speaker's actor has a branch for it in any state; if not, the speaker talks, as for any line, as the keeper does. Like the talk, the request lasts until the bodies next step, then both are asked to stand. Alaron reaches out as he takes the key (his actor's state 33).
  - Cut-scenes are as 1.0 has them.
- **`Battle`** hands over the enemies from `AddEnemy` (a thirteenth faults), the arena (above 27 becomes 0), the collision and animation bytes, whether the party can flee (not in cut-scenes), and the record's `battleFlag`, as the ending's `BattleSetup` (`AidynRenewed.Core.Combat`). In a conversation with someone who wanders, the original fights their party instead, under its flag C, in the arena their chunk's terrain chooses, with the bytes 0 and fleeing allowed (`BattleSetup.Against`, [combat](combat.md#in-and-out)), and a shop or training uses them as the keeper.
- **Where an ending leads** is decided in the core (`Transition.AfterScript`): the hours a camp heal or a camp scene passes, then the keeper's shop, their training, or the battle where the script ran. A keeper with no shop, or no keeper for training, is said instead, where the original would crash or open empty lists. Trek carries out a conversation's ending, the session a cut-scene's.
- **`CampHeal`** heals the party and ends the script, and eight hours pass. A camp scene (`rest`) adds eight more, so a camp heal in one would pass sixteen.
- **`AddMember` and `RemoveMember`** add and take away members, with their flags and kept sheets ([characters](characters.md#joining-and-leaving)). Retail ignores whether AddMember worked: the scripts check the party's size first. AddMember working in a conversation tells the runtime that the one talking leaves the map rather than going back to wandering ([wanderers](wanderers.md#joining)).

## Faults

Where the original crashes, or reads past its nodes, the runner ends the script as `EndDialogue` would and puts the reason in `Ending.Fault` (the debug build sets `borg13End` after its crash message). The faults are: a menu or case with no text, leaving a menu or case, a line with no link and no menu to go back to, an If with no link to take, a thirteenth enemy, command 49, a random line from a script that isn't there, and a walk through 10,000 nodes without showing anything.

Two scripts have problems in the data:

- **Node 24 of script 1040** (the harbour reunion, id 2192) is Dougal's line, and it lost its link to node 25 (its siblings have one, and an op 2 naming the next node). With Dougal in the party, the original reads a node past the end of the script and softlocks: the user saw 1.0 show an empty dialogue box with no portrait, which nothing moves on from, talking to Keelin in Port Saiid's inn with Dougal in the party. Play rarely gets there, since it needs a return to Port Saiid after Dougal joins. The runner ends the scene there, setting its done flag, so it doesn't softlock, but the reunion and Keelin's joining don't play. The user chose to leave it so and not link the node in the import: it waits for the bugfix mod ([ADR 0029](../adr/0029-content-fixes-wait-for-a-bugfix-mod.md)).
- **Trahern's conversation** (936) goes round nodes 64–69 forever once flags 67 and 84 are both set. Nothing sets 84, so it can't happen.

## Cut-scenes

`CutScene`, `CutSceneActor` and `CutSceneStage` in `AidynRenewed.Core.Scripts`, with `ScriptCameras` in `AidynRenewed.Core.Cameras` and `ScreenFade` at the core's root. From 1.0's machine code.

### The world it plays in

- **Starting.** A dialogue object (walked into or used) or a talker whose script is a cut-scene calls `Script.Start` (`0x8004ff80`), and `funcs0[1]` (`0x80051e04`) starts trek fading out, at 1/30 a tick. Trek runs on under the fade, clock and wanderers too, but the player's input is dead. Once the screen has been black three frames, trek frees its world (`TrekLoop.Free`, `0x800211a4`): the chunks, the wanderers and the player's body, keeping where the player stood, his facing and the camera's place.
- **The stage** is the dialogue object's staging chunk: every one of the 271 cut-scene objects has one. A talker's cut-scene stages in **the chunk the player is in** (`IMapObjectHost.Talk`, `0x800109fc`, passes `gameVars.mapShort1/2`), with reference points from 0, and sets the party's flag B. `DialogueModeInitPrescripted` (`0x800515a8`) loads the 3×3 around it, gated by the flags as trek does, **without monster parties' bodies** (the wanderers' load, the port's `Wanderers.Load`, returns in the dialogue state, `0x8000ef20`), and puts the camera at the stage's corner looking along X. Then the script's first nodes run, which spawn its actors and cut the camera (every cut-scene's first camera op is a cut), and the screen fades in over a second, 1/60 a tick.
- **Reference points** are looked up by id in the staging chunk's map alone (`FindReferncePoint`, `0x8000b6f0`), and nothing checks for a miss: the original reads address 0 and crashes. A chunk holds each staged scene's points in a block of its own, named for the scene (I08's `StageA_1156` block is 257–291, Edur's), and a dialogue object's base is its block's start less 1. Every point the 271 objects' scripts name is there.
- **Talkers' cut-scenes from base 0.** Each of the 9 talkers whose cut-scene names a point their chunk lacks also has a dialogue object for the same script, whose base finds them all: Edur in I08 (`0x0391`), Keelin in Terminor (`0x0382`) and seven more. Talking to the body runs the script from 0, so it reads the chunk's first block, another scene's points, and crashes where that block has no such id. Edur's object is walked into, 14 across, so the player meets it first. Keelin's is used with A, 1.2 from her body, so which one A reaches depends on his facing ([what A reaches](map-objects.md#what-a-reaches)); from her body, her scene crashes at her walk to point 34 whenever she isn't recruited (`TLB-FETCH` at `0x8004fbd4`, WalkTo's read of a point `FindReferncePoint` didn't find), after "Kisses." The port notes a miss in `CutScene.Problems` and skips the op, so she stays put while the scene ends.
- **Each tick** (dialogue mode's state 1): the actors within 30 of the camera's aim move and animate (`Bodies.Process`, `Body.Near`), then the script's cameras; the sky, clouds and weather go on, but **the clock stops**, and map objects, trigger triangles and chunk streaming don't run (`handleZoneEngineFrame` with no player). The near and far planes are 0.1 and 125 rather than 1 and 180 (`RenderZones`, `0x8000d66c`), which brings the fog in to about 21 units ([rendering](rendering.md#the-n64-material)).
- **The box** is the conversation's, and the player's A moves it on, but presses are dropped, not kept, while the screen fades, while the script waits (a walk, a timer, an animation or the camera: each of those also frees the box, so it disappears) and while a camera move runs (the box stays up). Each press the script takes asks the speaker's actor for request 4, talking (`Body.Talk`), unless the script played an animation (`funcs2[1]`, `0x80051ed4`). A conversation in trek does the same, and both press through `SpeakerTalk`.
- **No skip.** 1.0 has no R + A + Z + B: `CutScene.Control` (`0x80051378`) only tests B, which toggles a flag nothing reads.
- **The end** (`CutScene.CheckEnd`, `0x80051f4c`): the done flag is set, and the screen fades out over a second while actors and cameras carry on, then three frames at black. Every actor is freed, the stage goes (`CutScene.Free`, `0x8005169c`), and then, in order:
  1. **The script's ending kind**, which `Script.Start` (`0x8004ff80`) looked up in the endings table (`0x800DFE1C`) into `0x800dfe4c`: three scripts end the game and two lead to the cinematics. Each takes over, so no trek is built. The port imports the table as the base plugin's triggers on `script.ended` ([events](events.md#the-base-plugins)).
  2. **The chain** (`0x80051810`): while trek starts again at a named place (`Zones.SavedPlace`, `0x800d9092`, not −1) and the script returns to trek, the first dialogue object of the kept chunk's Borg9, in its order, that's tangible, holds the kept position within its radius and whose flag A holds (`MapObjects.Touches`, `0x800113bc`, whether it's active or not) starts at once, with `0x800dfe0c` set so trek queues no fade-out, and its cut-scene follows this one with one black frame between: no trek is built. Only the Goblin Ambush's end leaves a named place ([combat](combat.md#in-and-out)); going through a teleporter (`TeleportPlayer`, `0x8000bb54`), loading and a new game (`Title.Leave`) leave none. So Oriana's first cut-scene in the hut (#1181) leads straight into the next, in the blue area (#1183). The port keeps the place as `TrekLoop.AtPlace` and chains in `GameSession.EndCutScene`.
  3. **Trek** starts again where the player stood (`initGameTrek`): every map object gated again and every wanderer at its home node, the camera at its old place aimed at the player and swinging round quickly, and the screen fading in at 1/30 from black. Its first frame is drawn black, the fade-in raising it from the next (`ScreenFade.Step`, `0x8001f330`, runs before the mode), and that frame already walks into map objects: walking in has no fade term (`MapObjects.CanStart`, `0x80010d08`), so a tangible cut-scene where trek starts again cues on that first frame and fades out from black, and trek never shows. That's how the Goblin Ambush's battle, returning the party to Oriana's hut, goes into her first cut-scene. The port walks into objects through trek's fade-in but not through a teleport's or its fade-out (`TrekLoop.Tick`).
- **In the port** the session plays it (`GameSession.BeginCutScene`, `EndCutScene`) and the runtime draws it (`Game.CutScene.cs`); `--cutscene <id>` plays one at once. Trek frees its world, and the stage loads in a zone engine made from trek's (`ZoneEngine.Stage`): its own chunks, lights and rain about the cut-scene's camera, over trek's sounds and weather, with its own music. Trek's zone engine keeps its lights, dynamic ones included, for when trek resumes (`TrekLoop.Resume`).

### Actors

- **Spawning** (`CreateActor`): a body of the speaker's entity (its actor, radius, scale and height), set down at the reference point from on high (mode 0 of the original's `0x80011a64`, from `DialoguePreCallback` at `0x8004f4e4`; [movement](movement.md#setting-a-body-down)); the point's own height doesn't count. It faces the original's default, (−0.71, −0.71), and is kept 0.05 inside the staging chunk. The original's flags on it: 0x1000, whose only reader is `SetFacing` (`0x80015910`), so **its first turn snaps**, however late it comes, and keeps any goal (`Body.SnapNextFace`); and 0x400, which keeps it out of the push loop, so actors don't push each other. Spawning one again leaves the first body standing, as the original's pointer is overwritten. Alaron is in a cut-scene only if the script spawns him. The actor is the entity's own (`EntityDB::GetBorg7`): Phelan in the final scene (`aidyn:dialogue/0x04FE`) is entity `0x0230`, whose actor `0x2918` is his only one, the one-legged model, standing in its idle and lying in the states the script asks for (20 and 21, then 9 to die); nothing turns or lowers him but the animations, as he's set down like any spawn.
- **A spawn over no ground** stays where the point is, height and all (`AllocPlayer` at the point's X, Y and Z, `0x8004f4c8`, then the set-down's tries to the side), and falls, as any body near the camera's aim does, until it's 30 from the aim and stops stepping. Back in Port Saiid from the Namer's Isle (`aidyn:dialogue/0x0430`), Alaron is spawned at point 43, about 10 above the ground around it and over a hole in D15's collision. The script's one camera (point 35, aimed at point 11) has him 33° off its axis, outside a 4:3 picture's 28.9° but inside 16:9's 36.4°, so on the console he falls unseen, and only a wide window shows him appear in the air and drop through the ground. The port does as 1.0 does.
- **Turning** (`FaceRefPoint`) to a point, 10° a frame where it stands after the first; nothing waits for it, but a turning actor counts as steered (below).
- **Walking and running** (`WalkTo`, `RunTo`): `SetAiDest` to the point's X and Z, within **1**. The script waits, and **a walk's wait ends when no actor has been steered for 30 ticks** and no camera move runs (`Bodies.Process`, `0x800136c4`): there's no "arrived". An actor beyond 30 of the camera's aim isn't stepped, so it never walks and the wait ends 30 ticks on. An actor that comes no nearer for **300 ticks** is put at its goal and set down (`MoveTo`, `0x80015a7c`, `Body.GiveUp` `0x80015c28`; 30 in combat, never in trek): the 30 is the wait's rule, not this one. While the screen fades, a steered actor stands (unless it's dying, below), though its steering, and so its count, goes on (`Actor::Move`, `Body.Faded`). The steering is once a frame ([wanderers](wanderers.md)), so where an actor comes to rest depends on it. **Only the actor's root motion moves it** (`Bodies.Process`, `0x80012784`, at `0x80013318`–`0x80013400`: the root's step this tick times the body's scale along its heading); the steering only sets the stick, which sets the request and the heading (`Body.StepTowardGoal`, `0x800159c8`; `Body.ApplyInput`, `0x80014fec`), and nothing gives a body a speed of its own. So an actor with no walk stands where it is, turning to its goal, until the 300 ticks put it there: Pochanargat (`aidyn:actor/0x32B0`, whose requests 2 and 3 lead back to her idle, and none of whose animations drives the root) walking to point 3 after "It is time this miserable world was rid of you!" in her scene (`aidyn:dialogue/0x04FD`) jumps there five seconds on. The port does as 1.0 does. Making her slide would be a departure the user would choose: a speed for a steered body whose state's animation doesn't move its root, in `Body.Animate` or `Advance`, at a pace someone picks, scaled by her 1.179, or a plugin giving her actor a walking state, which her scene has no animation for. The user chose to leave her as 1.0 has her (Oct 24, 2026): a mod could give her a walk animation, or change the cut-scene, once the editor can.
- **A walk waits behind a talk.** The count of no progress isn't reset as the actor gets nearer (`+0x25c` is zeroed only by `Body.WalkTo`, `0x80015724`), and it runs while an animation that takes no interrupt holds the actor. The press that brings up an actor's line asks it to talk, and some talks are long and uninterruptible: Shamsuk's (actor `0x294A`, request 4 to state 10, 360 ticks, whose only branch is at its end). In his tower (`aidyn:dialogue/0x0494`) the press that shows "Finally! I was beginning to think…" (node 44, or "Squire?" before it if that press came within 6 seconds) starts his talk, and moving on from node 44 sends him walking to point 9 while it plays. Move on within about a second and a half and he stands through most of it, the 300 run out once he's under way, and `Body.GiveUp` puts him on point 9 itself, from where his walk's step carries him a quarter past it. The close-ups that follow (nodes 64, 72 and 116: `CameraCutToActor` Alaron, `CameraOn` point 3, the camera at his eyes looking down at the ground point beyond Shamsuk) then have him at the left edge of the picture rather than left of its middle. That's 1.0's rules: the console does the same for a player as quick.
- **Into another actor.** The user saw the original refuse a walk to a point inside another actor in the Fire Lord's scene (`0x048F`): the walker stops at the other and turns, or goes off another way. The port's actors don't push each other and walk where the script says, which the user judged less faithful but better, and chose to keep. What stops them in 1.0 isn't settled: its push loop skips bodies flagged 0x400 (`Bodies.Process`, `0x80013514` and `0x80013584`), which every cut-scene actor is.
- **Animations** (`PlayAnimation`): the speaker's actor is asked for the request (`ApplyStimulus`), which it keeps for the original's frame and the next, then stands (`CutScene.StandActors`, `0x80051e2c`, the debug build's `0x80059b50`, asks every actor to stand each frame unless the script animated it). A request that doesn't interrupt its state so never plays. **The wait is a timer**: after the tick it's seen, it lasts the length of the state the actor is then in (`aniLength`), counted from that tick. With no actor on the stage (its spawn missed), nothing waits. The box is gone through the wait, so A can't cut it short.
- **A death holds** (request 9). `Bodies.Process` (`0x80013200`–`0x800132D4`) does it for every body, not only combat's: once the actor has entered a state for 9 (the body's `+0x18`) and 9 is no longer asked for, it sets `+0x77C`, and from then the actor plays to the end of the state it's in and stops there, its state machine no longer stepped, whatever it's asked for after. Nothing clears the flag but a body's making (`Player.Reset`, `0x80011C90`). No other request holds: the rest go back to idle by their states' branches. And the stick of a steered body asks it to stand only if it isn't dying (`Body.ApplyInput`, `0x80014FEC`, also while the screen fades), so an actor still turning on the spot keeps a death it's asked for. The port does both: `Body.Mortal` wraps each cut-scene actor's animation, and `Body.StandUnlessDying` is the stick's first word. Trek never asks a body for 9, and combat keeps its own ([combat](combat.md#an-attack)). So in the Fire Lord's scene at the end of Shamsuk's Tower (`aidyn:dialogue/0x048F`) he lies where he fell, though his actor's death (state 6 of `aidyn:actor/0x2FBA`) goes back to idle for any request, and Niesen dies even when A on "I remembered..." asks it while he's still turning to Alaron.
- **A death the actor's state takes no part in is lost.** Each press asks the new line's speaker to talk (above), and Niesen's talking (state 15 of `aidyn:actor/0x2BB3`, 241 ticks) has no branch for 9. So with Rheda in the party, A pressed on "Rheda, I see a place..." (node 230) within four seconds of it showing asks for his death while he still talks: it's never taken, neither the fall nor its cry plays, and the wait lasts the talk's 241 ticks before the fade. Waited out, he falls. The user saw 1.0 do both, from a save written into Niesen's room: A the moment the line came up left him standing a few seconds, with no fall or cry, before the fade, and waiting let him fall with his cry. The port does the same. His cry, and the Fire Lord's, is the request 9 key of Alaron's profile (`aidyn:sequence/0x06EA` at the first frame), which every cut-scene actor keeps ([audio](audio.md#bodies-sounds)).
- **Held items** (`ShowItem`, `HideItem`): the item's model (`ItemTables.ItemModels`, `0x8006D018`, imported as `Model` on weapons, shields and six items: see [db](../formats/db.md)) on the speaker's scene's locator 0 (`AttachItemToPlayer`, `0x80014BA0`, slot 0), turned by the locator model's rotation and scaled with the body, drawn even while the body is hidden. An item without one, such as the two spells script `0x0434` shows, leaves what's held as it is. It's the slot combat puts a weapon in, drawn the same way ([combat](combat.md#what-the-bodies-hold)).
- **The fade op** (`FadeOut`) dips: down over a second and back up over another by itself (fade mode 3), while the script carries on. No cut-scene uses it.
- **Command 53**, once near the start of every cut-scene, does nothing: it only takes its node's effect slot.

### In the port

`CutScene` carries out each cue on its `CutSceneStage` as it comes. Two traps:

- **A wait waits on the stage,** not on the cue that started it: on the actors being steered or the cameras moving. So scripts could share a stage and run side by side.
- **The script's cameras keep their place by the game's ticker** (`GameState.Ticker`), as trek's camera objects do; a move is stepped every tick until it's freed.

The session's `Tick` hears the actors from the camera and steps the stage's sounds, as `CutScene.PlayFrame` (`0x80050f50`) runs `HandleZoneEngineFrame`; the plain `Tick` the tests use steps the cut-scene alone. A real-data test plays every cut-scene a dialogue object or a talker starts to its end in a new game.
