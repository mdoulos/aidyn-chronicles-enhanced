# Audio (roadmap 1D)

How the port plays sound, and what plays which sound when. [The audio format](../formats/audio.md) has the samples and the sequences' layout. Addresses are 1.0's.

## What's ported

- **The player** is the core's `AudioEngine` (`AidynRenewed.Core.Audio`): the original's sequencer and voice manager, stepped on the original's 1000 µs clock at any output rate, mixing 32 voices to 16-bit stereo. It plays a `Sequence`, which `Sequence.Load` builds from a sequence record, its `.dcm` events and its samples' WAVs. `aidyn-renewed audio` renders with it.
- **The game's side** is the core's `GameAudio`, which tells the player what to play (the music, one-shots and the volumes, from the `SessionSettings` it's handed each tick, [ADR 0023](../adr/0023-the-core-is-handed-its-settings-each-tick.md)), with `SoundBank` loading sequences from the plugins as they're first asked for. Everything takes a lock, so the runtime's `AudioOutput` mixes on a thread of its own, about 60 ms ahead, into a Godot generator stream, and the game's hitches as chunks load don't starve it. The runtime steps `GameAudio` every tick, in the pause menu too.
- **What asks for sounds:** trek and a cut-scene's stage ask for their chunks' music and hold the change through fades; `MapSounds` plays the loaded chunks' sound objects, `AudioKeys` bodies' footsteps and voices, `WeatherSounds` the rain and thunder, and map objects and scripts play one-shots. The pause menu cues its sounds to its host (`IPauseMenuHost`, `MenuSoundCue`), and the runtime plays them.
- **Data:** the music is on the map set and chunk records, the sounds engine code names in `aidyn:sounds/0` (`SoundsRecord`, with the body kinds' footsteps), and the audio keys, weapons' sounds and spells' casts on the entity, weapon and spell records.

## The driver

Two players run on libultra's synthesizer, each called every 1000 µs of audio (`_timeToSamplesNoRound`: 44 samples at 44,095 Hz), so a sequencer millisecond is 0.998 ms. The game asks for 44,100 Hz (`Audio.Start`, `0x8008F680`), which NTSC's clock makes 44,095. The synthesizer has 32 voices, makes 16-bit stereo, and has an effects bus whose mix is always 0, so there's no reverb.

- **The sequencer** (`AudioEngine.StepPlayers`, `0x80090C94`) steps up to 32 sequences at once (`AudioEngine.Play`, `0x800907A4`, fails beyond that). Each counts calls up to its row period, then reads a row. It stops a sequence that has played past its duration (-1 for none), and at the stream's end either stops it or goes back to the loop position. When a looping sequence ends, every voice is cut and it starts again a row later.
- **The voice manager** (`AudioEngine.UpdateVoices`, `0x800900E4`) carries the sequencer's changes to the synthesizer on its next call, and frees a voice that plays once when it reaches its sample's end. A new note takes a free voice. With none free, a sequence started with a non-zero priority takes the oldest; otherwise the note is dropped.
- **Volume:** a note's volume byte is `trackVolume × sequenceVolume >> 4`, where the sequence's is the byte it was started with (and `AudioEngine.SetVolume` changes). The synthesizer gets `min(byte << 6, 0x7FFF)`, and its envelope mixer squares that (`v × v >> 15`). So full volume, 15 × 255 >> 4 = 239, is 0.218 of full scale on each voice. Changes glide over 1000 µs. There's no envelope: notes start with a 1000 µs glide up and stop with a hard cut.
- **Pan:** a sequence also has a pan, 0x80 the middle. A track's pan is scaled into a window around it, `255 - 2|P - 0x80|` wide, so at the middle the track's own pan holds and towards either end every track narrows to the sequence's. The synthesizer takes `pan >> 1`, at most 127, and pans by equal power (`eqpower`, `0x800E5010`, `32767 × cos(k × π / 254)`).
- **Where the port differs:** the sequencer's changes reach the voices at once rather than on the voice manager's next call, up to a millisecond sooner. libultra's four-tap resampler is approximated with cubic interpolation. And where the original reads past a sample's end (one instrument's loop runs past its sample), the port plays silence.

## Not played yet

What makes a sound that the port doesn't do yet, each waiting for what plays it:

- **The title screen's** music (0x0632 at volume 0xA5) and its menu's sounds (`IntroMenu`: the cursor, A, B and Start), [ui](ui.md#the-title-screen). The port plays the music as the zone engine's at the music volume, which the original sets it to every frame.
- **Cheat codes:** 0x0718 accepted, 0x0721 rejected. The cheat menu isn't ported.
- **Dialogue entities' bodies:** a wanderer that's a dialogue entity has no audio keys in the port. The original looks its profile up by its position in its own list, into the entities' profiles, which isn't settled.
- **The menus' own sounds:** a parchment popup opening (0x074D) and the crafting and healer lists closing (0x074E); and the save and load screens' own A and B, 0x0740 as a save has loaded (`PauseMenu.OnLoaded`, `0x80048E18`) and 0x0741 going back to the options (`0x8003FF30`). The port plays only the pause menu's for those presses.

## Music

**One piece of background music plays at a time** (`Bgm.Update`, `0x8000E570`, every frame of trek, a conversation, a cut-scene or combat, but not through a fade, while `ScreenFade.State` isn't 0). The request (`Bgm.Requested`, `0x800D90A2`) is 0 for nothing new, −1 to stop, and otherwise a Borg12. When it's another piece, the old one stops and the new one starts from its beginning, looped, centred, at `trunc(music volume × 255)`; the same piece plays on. There's no crossfade and no ducking. The volume is sent again every frame, and the pause menu's frame (`PauseMenu.Frame`, `0x80048744`) sends it too, so the Options slider acts at once. The pause menu doesn't take requests.

**The request is made** by `Zones.SetMapSet` (`0x80026264`) on every teleport, a door's or a script's, and on every crossing into another chunk, which the original handles as a teleport without a fade (`ConfirmPlayerWithinZone`, `Zones.QueueChunkShift`). So walking out of the chunks with music cuts it, and walking back starts it over. A door's music changes as its fade ends. It asks for −1, then the set's rule:

- **The Overworld's** lists don't apply on the title screen (`Zones.Mode` 2, `0x800D90A6`), whose scenery is Overworld chunks.
- **Cut-scenes:** cueing one keeps the music playing (`FreeZoneEngine(1)`), and its stage asks for its own (`DialogueModeInitPrescripted`, `0x800515a8`: `InitZoneEngine` asks for trek's map set and chunk, then `CutScene.LoadStage`, `0x80051984`, for the stage's, through `Zones.SetMapSet`). The request waits out the stage's fade-in, as `Bgm.Update` does every fade, and then takes over: the same piece plays on, and a stage whose map set and chunk have none stops it. So the Wizard's School's music (the Jundar interiors' K01, 0x061C) plays on through Ardra's healing (#1926, staged in K01), and trek's first tick after it walks into the dream world's cut-scene (#1981, the same spot), staged in the Misc interiors' C03, which has none: the school's music plays through that stage's fade-in and stops once the screen is up. Trek asks again as it comes back, so it starts over. The port's stage holds the music through its own fade (`CutScene.Tick`); without that, trek's hold from its fade-out lasted the whole cut-scene and no stage changed the music. Without the Expansion Pak, `InitZoneEngine` also stops trek's piece at once when the request differs (`Bgm.StopIfNoPak`, `0x8000e528`); the port plays as with the Pak. Leaving trek any other way (loading, a fight, game over) stops it (`Bgm.Stop(1)`), so it starts over.
- **Battles:** trek's music is cut as trek goes for a battle (`TrekLoop.Free`, `0x800211A4`, `FreeZoneEngine(0)`), at the black of its fade out, and the arena's piece starts once the arena has faded in. Every way out of a battle, won, lost, fled or the Shadow's, frees the arena (`Combat.Exit`, `0x80023060`, `FreeZoneEngine(0)`, `0x8000E6C8`, `Bgm.Stop(1)`, `0x8000E4C4`): a cut, not a fade nor a request of −1, which stops the sequence and forgets the piece, and leaves the one-shots. A win's exit comes on the first frame after the fade out, so the music plays on full through it and is cut about three frames into the black, before the post-battle window's scroll sounds (checkpoint 12, fading out, still has `Bgm.Playing` 0x05B4; 13 and 14, in the windows, have none). The windows (game mode 6, `PauseMenu.Frame` alone) never call `Bgm.Update` and have no map sounds, as `TrekLoop.Free` and `Combat.Exit` each cleared them (`Audio.ClearSounds(1)`, `0x8004E738`), so they're silent but for their own sounds ([ui](ui.md#after-a-battle)); the loot window's close builds trek again, whose music starts from its beginning once trek has faded in. The port's cut is `GameAudio.StopMusic`, in `GameSession.EndBattle` and `TrekLoop.BeginBattle`.
- **The title** plays 0x0632 (0x05D9 without the Pak) at volume 0xA5, looped (`Title.StartMusic`, `0x8001B0B0`). The credits play 0x05D9 at the music volume, looped, with the sound and music volumes at 0 under it, so trek's music and sounds go on silently, and stop it at once as they end ([ui](ui.md#the-credits); the port's is in `Game.Credits.cs`).
- **The cinematics** play a sequence per shot (the cinematic records name them), looped, at the louder of the two volumes (`Cinematic.StartShot`, `0x80053E4C`). Before one, the original clears every sound and stops the music, asking for it again after, so it starts over; each shot stops its own sequence as it ends. The port plays them so ([cinematics](cinematics.md#playing)).

## Sound map objects

A map's sound objects (`SoundObject`) are played by `ProcessAudioBubbles` (`0x8004ECA0`) every frame, for all nine loaded chunks, as each chunk's Borg9 loads them (`Audio.StartChunk`, `0x8004E58C`), into 82 slots (`Audio.Init`, `0x8004DF08`). `Audio.AddSound` doesn't check the count, and the 3×3 around the Overworld's H13 has 84, so the original writes past its slots there; the port drops the rest. `StartChunk` skips an object only for the Expansion Pak (flag 0x40 without it, 0x20 with it), and music objects on the title screen (`MapSounds.SkipsMusic`). The port's `MapSounds` follows it, and keeps each sound up as a `KeptSound`, as it does one-shots (`Audio.UpdateSound` is one function in the original); `AudioLevels` works out the loudness, volume and pan bytes for map sounds and bodies, which differ only in clamping.

- **Inside its radius** of the player (3D, `≤`), a sound's loudness is `1 − (distance from what the camera looks at) / radius`, unclamped (`Camera.Listener+0xC`; `+0` is the eye). A sound with falloff takes it as its fade; a `NoFalloff` one fades in instead, by `delta / 90` a frame up to 1. Its pan, unless it's `NoPan`, is `(f.x × d.z − f.z × d.x) × (1 − loudness)` across the ground, with `f` the camera's facing and `d` the way from the eye to the sound, both normalised: positive to the right (`Audio.Pan`, `0x8004EB1C`), unclamped, so a sound on the left comes from the left speaker, as the user heard in the original.
- **Outside it,** its fade falls by `delta / 90` a frame while it's above 0, and it's kept up meanwhile. At 0 a playing sound stops. Coming back starts it over.
- **Keeping it up** (`Audio.UpdateSound`, `0x8004E900`), every frame: the volume byte is `clamp(trunc(fade × the object's volume × 255), 0, 255)`, then `trunc(that × (Music ? music volume : sound volume))`, and the pan's `clamp(trunc((pan + 1) × 0.5 × 255), 0, 255)`, so a pan of 0 is 127. A playing sound gets them, and a duration of −1. One that isn't playing has its gate read (`0x8004EA10`) whether or not it needs starting, which brings the flag network up to date when the gate's flag is derived ([flags](flags.md#when-the-network-comes-up-to-date)); one that needs starting then starts, if its flags hold, looped if it's `Loop`, with a duration of 2000 ms, so one the game stops keeping up dies within two seconds. A `Loop` sound still needs starting after, so one that ends starts again; any other plays once each time the player comes into its radius.
- **`Repeat`:** a countdown falls by `delta` a frame inside the radius. At 0 a repeating sound is cut, needs starting, and waits `(short)trunc((MinWait + Float(MaxWait − MinWait)) × 60)` ticks again (`RandomExtensions.Float`, the main stream). It starts at 0, so a repeating sound starts at once.
- **The initial pan** (`+0x2C`) is 0 in every object.
- **Music objects** play across chunks: when a chunk unloads, its playing music becomes a one-shot of two ticks (`Audio.PersistMusic`), which the next chunk to load the same sequence takes over (`Audio.FindPersistentMusic`), so it plays on without a break. There are 14, in a few Overworld chunks and map sets 6, 8 and 12, and each needs the Expansion Pak.
- **Around them:** the pause menu keeps the sounds going with the player and the camera held still, a delta of 1 a frame, and frees none. Under the post-battle windows there are none to keep: trek's went as the battle began, and the runtime streams trek's chunks again only once the windows have closed (`GameSession.StreamsTrek`). A conversation is trek's own frame. Cueing a cut-scene frees every sound but music (`Audio.ClearSounds(1)`), and the stage's chunks load their own. A teleport moves the sounds with the chunks (`Audio.ShiftSounds`) and frees those of chunks left behind.

## Sound effects

One-shots go through `Audio.PlaySound` (`0x8004E150`): a sequence, a pan, a gain, a lifetime in ticks and a delay. Each is a sound object of its own in one of 16 slots, `NoFalloff` and `NoPan`, always there, with a radius of 1000 (`Audio.OneShotRadius`, `0x800D1D14`); with all 16 taken it's dropped. It keeps the sound volume at the call as its own volume and starts its fade at the gain, which rises to 1 over 90 ticks. It waits its delay, starts on the next `ProcessAudioBubbles`, plays once, and holds its slot until its lifetime is up (`lifetime + delay` from the call), when it's stopped. Its volume is `trunc(trunc(fade × the sound volume at the call × 255) × the sound volume now)`, clamped. In the pause menu, lifetimes count its frames. The port's are `GameAudio.Play`'s, each a `KeptSound`.

## Bodies' sounds

Each body's footsteps, voice and weapon come from the **audio keys** (`AudioKeys`, the DB table at ROM `0x1FE6780`, loaded by `AudioKeys.Load`, `0x80053000`). `Bodies.Process` (`0x80012784`) checks them every tick for every body with an actor within 50 of what the camera looks at (`AudioKeys.ProcessPlayer`, `0x80053560`), in trek, conversations, cut-scenes and combat.

- **The table** is three u32 offsets (profiles at 0x18, keys at 0x6F8, key indices at 0xF98) and three u16 counts (220, 276, 560). A profile is 8 bytes: a u16 kind, a u16 count of keys, a u16 start into the key indices, and a zero. A key is 8 bytes: a u16 `frac`, a request byte, a count of sound ids (0, 1 or 4) and four id bytes. A key index is a u16 into the keys.
- **A body's profile** is its entity's position in the entity list (`GetIdIndex`, body `+0x758`). The player and cut-scene actors keep `Player.Reset`'s (`0x80011C90`) default, 153, Alaron's; wanderers and combatants get their entity's. Position 220 (Dracovern) reads past the 220 profiles into key 0, which is kind 0: silent.
- **When a key fires.** Each tick the actor steps (its frame runs 1 to the animation's length, the core's `ActorStateMachine.Clock`), and when its frame after the step is 1 the body latches its request (`Bodies.Process`, `0x8001329C`: body `+0x14` into `+0x16`), which `AudioKeys.ProcessPlayer` gets (`0x8001341C`). Combat's one-shot requests (the attacks, the hit, the dodge, the death) are asked for through every tick of their frame (`Combat.AfterFrame` lets them go once a frame), and an actor takes only a request its state has a branch for (`ActorStateMachine.SetRequest`, `0x80096530`, falling back to its default, 0, after every tick), so the tick after a state is entered for one counts as a change of request and doesn't advance: the frame stays 1 and the latch keeps the one-shot's request. A key at frame 1 (the death's, the helped's) so plays on both ticks, into the body's two slots, a tick apart. A key fires on the tick its request is the latched one and the frame equals `(u16)(int)(frac / 65500f × length)` in single precision, or 1 if that's 0 (65500.0 at `0x800D205C`). So a frac of 65500 is the last frame. It fires again only if the frame holds on the next tick. `AudioKeys.PlayEntityKey` (`0x800532BC`) plays the first key that matches: one of its ids at random (`Rng.Below`, the main stream), into the 126 voice sequences (`0x800E02A8`); a key with no ids plays the weapon's sound instead.
- **The kind** (0 for 3 profiles, 1 for 6, 2 for 194, 3 for 17) sets the footsteps' loudness, × 0.5, 0.75 or 1.0 (doubles at `0x800D2070`, `0x800D2080`), and the footsteps' set: kinds 1 and 2 the people's, 3 the heavy bodies'. Kind 0 plays nothing at all.
- **Footsteps** (`AudioKeys.PlaySurfaceSteps`, `0x800530DC`) come from the step sets (`0x800DFFE8`, ROM `0xE0BE8`): 26 surfaces × sneak, walk and run, a byte each, indexing 48 footstep keys (`0x800DFE60`, ROM `0xE0A60`, 8 bytes each as above, entry 0 empty), all at frac 65500 with four step ids, for request 2, 3 or 25. Step ids index 96 sequences (`0x800E0128`, ROM `0xE0D28`). The surface is the last one the body's sphere touched that's 3 or more (body `+0x102`, updated before anything else each tick), so walls and leaving the ground don't change it. For people: sand and default 0x067E–0x0681, snow 0x068A–0x068D, grass 0x0649–0x064C, mud 0x0679–0x067C, sizzling 0x0682–0x0685, cave 0x0644–0x0647, wood 0x069F–0x06A2, echoing wood 0x06A3–0x06A6, stone 0x068E–0x0691, echoing stone 0x0692–0x0695, the great echoing stone 0x0696–0x0699, water 0x069B–0x069E, and sneaking 0x0686–0x0689 on all but water. Heavy bodies don't sneak: default, grass and sand 0x0657–0x065A, snow 0x0660–0x0663, mud 0x065C–0x065F, cave and the echoing stones 0x0668–0x066B, wood 0x066D–0x0670, echoing wood 0x06A7–0x06AA, stone 0x0664–0x0667, and sizzling and water as people's. The see-through surfaces sound as their plain ones.
- **The weapon's sound** (`AudioKeys.WeaponSound`, `0x800876E8`) is only in combat (`Combat.IndexOfBody` searches only in game mode 2): the wielded weapon's row of a table by `WeaponsWithModels` position (`0x800D44D0`, ROM `0xD50D0`, 8 bytes a row, its first two u16s alike), a voice id, such as 2 (0x06AD) for axes and 107 for staves. No weapon, or one without a model, is silent. **A spell's cast** (`AudioKeys.PlaySpellCast`, `0x800534BC`) plays its sequence from 59 by spell position (`0x800E04A0`, ROM `0xE10A0`) on the first tick the caster's request is 5 after combat's `Player.SetCastSound`.
- **Loudness** (`Audio.VolumeAndPan`, `0x8004EB90`): `(u8)(int)((1 − distance from what the camera looks at / 15) × 255)`, with nothing played at 0. It isn't clamped: past 15 the int's low byte wraps, so a body from about 15.1 to 50 away is loud again, a sawtooth falling off to 1 at 30, as the emulator bears out. Footsteps take the kind's factor, truncated, and skip at 0; then everything plays at `(u8)(int)(byte × sound volume)`, once. The pan is map sounds', with `1 − loudness` being `distance / 15`.
- **In the port,** `AudioKeys` wraps each body's animation (`AudioKeys.Sounding`), so the keys are checked on every step of it: the player's, the wanderers' and a cut-scene's actors', which have Alaron's keys as the original's do. In a battle, a key with no sound of its own plays the weapon's (`BodyAudio.Weapon`), and the cast's sound plays from `BodyAudio.Cast` ([combat](combat.md#what-spells-look-like)). Combat holds a one-shot request through a frame (`Combatant.OneShot`) and an actor takes only a request its state has a branch for (`ActorStateMachine.Taken`), as above: without both, the latch takes idle a tick later and only the frame-1 keys sound. No visual, burst or impact has a sound of its own. The port's full run ([movement](movement.md), request 26) latches as the run, 3, so it steps and voices as the run does.
- **A battle's sounds** are the music, the weather and the bodies' keys alone: no combat file calls an audio function. The attacker's attack key (requests 12 to 18, none with ids of their own) plays its weapon's sound partway through the swing, hit or miss alike (Alaron's Club at frame 41 of 111; a rat's bite is silent, as its weapon has no model); the target's hit (7) and dodge (8) keys play its grunt or its dodge from four ids; an ally helped plays 10's (frame 1), a death 9's (frame 1), a cast 5's. A shield's block is no event of its own: a blow that does nothing is a dodge. The weapon table's rows are `{a, a, 0, 0}`: the last two are never read, and one of the first two is picked with `Rng.Range(0, 1)` on the main stream. Spells' visuals, arrows, flasks, the level up and the victory make no sound in the battle.
- **Two slots a body.** `AllocPlayerAudio` (`0x80012610`) alternates between them, stopping what was in the one it takes, and plays once, without a time limit, centred where the body was: the sound doesn't follow it.

## Volumes

- **The sound and music volumes** are floats, 1.0 and 0.65 in a new game, which scale the byte each sequence is played at. The port's settings keep them in hundredths (`Settings.SoundVolume`, 100; `MusicVolume`, 65).
- **One-shots take the sound volume twice:** `Audio.PlaySound` stores it as the sound's own volume and `Audio.UpdateSound` multiplies by it again; some pass it as their gain too, a starting fade. [User-checks](../user-checks.md) asks whether that's audible.
- **Nothing has priority.** Every sequence is played with priority 0, so a note finding all 32 voices busy is dropped, and a 33rd sequence doesn't play.
