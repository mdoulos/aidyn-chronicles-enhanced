# Wanderers: monster parties' bodies (M2)

`AidynEnhanced.Core.Trek` (`Wanderers`, `Wanderer`) and `AidynEnhanced.Core.Movement` (`Body`, `Crowd`). Ported from `wander.cpp`, `PlayerMove.cpp` (`Actor::SetAiDest`, `SetFacing`, `MoveTo`, `Move`) and `PlayerData.cpp` (`Bodies.Process`, `PlayerShadowAlpha`, `renderPlayers`). What a monster party holds is in [maps](../formats/maps.md#map-objects-in-the-base-plugin-06).

A monster party is an NPC to talk to, if it has a dialogue, or a group of enemies the player sees as its leader. Either way it's one body in the world, which wanders between wander nodes.

## Bodies as their chunk loads

- **When.** As a chunk loads, each of its monster parties that's active by its flags ([map-objects](map-objects.md#a-chunks-objects-at-run-time)) and not remembered as beaten (a party with no flag to mark it beaten, for three chunks crossed or teleports: [saves](saves.md#map-objects-memory)) gets a body (the port's `Wanderers.Load`; `AllocWanderer`); as it goes, they go (`FreeWandererFromZone`). The original skips this in dialogue mode and on the title screen.
- **No limit.** The original has room for 39 at once in trek (`WanderHead`) and crashes past it, at least in the debug build. A new game's 3×3 around Overworld H13 holds 44 parties that would get one. The port gives every party a body, as the user chose: mods will add their own. Whether the original leaves some out rather than crashing is unsettled ([user-checks](../user-checks.md)).
- **Its look** is the party's entity's body ([actors](../formats/actors.md)): its actor, its collision radius, its scale and its height. The body is drawn at its scale, its animations carry it that much farther, and bodies push each other with spheres that much bigger; its sphere against the world keeps the radius as it is.
- **Where.** At the party's position, set down near the party's height, its sphere's centre there, then from on high (`AllocWanderer` calls the original's set-down, `0x80011a64`, in mode 1 at `0x8000f1d4`; [movement](movement.md#setting-a-body-down)).
- **Its home node** must be one that wanderers walk to; every party's is. It takes the node's values (`WanderGetNextNode`), and if it's further from the node than the node's radius it sets off for it (`Wanderer.Resume`, `0x8000f5b4`), **walking if the party has flag 0x2 and running otherwise**. The flag is the party's: 1.0's code reads `voxelObjs[VoxelIndex] + 0x58`. 990 of the 1,079 parties walk; the 89 that run are the game's (a man in Port Saiid and a few monsters).
- **Kept in its chunk.** A body other than the player's is kept 0.05 inside its chunk's edges (`Body.ChunkBounds`), since the original keeps it in its chunk's coordinates.

## Bodies moving

A `Body` is the original's `playerData` for a body other than Alaron's, stepped each tick as `Bodies.Process` steps it:

- **Steering** (`MoveTo`). A body sent somewhere (`SetAiDest`) points its stick at the goal, at half tilt to walk or 0.95 to run, and the stick turns it 20° a frame as the player's does. The original routes the stick through the camera's turn and back, which cancels out. The stick follows the goal only while the body comes nearer than it's been, by 0.01; otherwise it keeps its last way, so a body that starts facing away walks a short curve. Within the goal's radius it stops. In trek a body that can't get nearer keeps trying; in a cut-scene the original gives up after 300 ticks and puts it there (see [scripts](scripts.md#actors)).
- **Once a frame.** `Body.StepTowardGoal` (`0x800159c8`) runs once a frame from `Bodies.Process`: the 0.01 is since the last frame's test (`0x800cdcc8`), the frame's `delta` is added to the ticks of no progress (`+0x25c`, `0x80015aa4`), and the radius is tested against where the frame began, the stick left for the rest of it and only let go as the next frame's call finds the goal gone (`0x80015b2c`). So a body walks on for a frame after it comes within its radius, and whether its walk's last step was entered under the walk, and so carries it on ([actors](../formats/actors.md)), turns on that. The port steers once a 30 fps frame (`Body.Steer`, every `Cadence.FrameTicks` from the step after it sets off) and turns every tick by the frame's share. Tested every tick, a walk came to rest a step short and its slow start counted about twice as much toward giving up: Shamsuk's walk to his tower's point 9 (`aidyn:dialogue/0x0494` node 44) ended 1 short of it rather than the half the user's screenshot of 1.0 shows, which framed him right of the close-ups' middle rather than left.
- **Turning** (`SetFacing`) turns it 10° a frame where it stands (`MoveTo`'s `0x3E32B7FE`), half what it turns on the move. The turn ends once the heading is within rounding of the goal: compared exactly, about a third of headings never finish turning, and a talker that turned to the player would never wander again. A body with neither a goal nor a turn lets go of its stick, and keeps whatever animation it was last asked for. A body stopped (`ResetMoveQueue`) is still steered (flag 0x2) for one more step, which asks it to stand: a hunter giving up must not let go at once, or it keeps playing its run and its root motion carries it past the leash.
- **Then** the world's collision, the push down on plain collision, and its animation's root motion, as the player's ([movement](movement.md)). Bodies don't set off trigger triangles.
- **Only near the camera.** In trek, bodies more than 30 from the camera's aim don't move or animate at all (`Body.StepDistance`, `PlayerHandler.float_0x68`; 128 in combat).

## Bodies pushing each other

After each body moves, the player first, it's pushed out of the others in its chunk within 3 in height (`Bodies.Process`). The push is across the ground only, straight away from the other until they touch, with each sphere at its scale, and the body's velocity bounces and dampens as off a wall. The other doesn't move.

- **The first push starts the turn over** from the second body, not the first, so the player, who is first, isn't weighed again. The body that pushed is weighed again, and touching counts, so whether the body stays or goes back depends on rounding.
- **A second push puts the body back** where it began the tick.
- **The nearest body** to each, across the ground, is kept for A (the body's `+0x108`). On a miss the original measures it with the other's radius at this body's scale, and so does the port.

## Drawing them

`WandererView` (a `BodyView`, as a cut-scene actor's is) draws each body's actor at its feet, facing its heading. Bodies show within 20 of the camera's aim and fade over the last 2 units (`PlayerShadowAlpha`). The original also skips those more than 5 from the aim that are outside its view, which only saves drawing. Their shadows show within 10 of the aim, measured from the body's position in its own chunk's coordinates against the aim in the player's chunk's, so a body in a neighbouring chunk seldom has one ([movement](movement.md#shadows)).

## Wandering, noticing and chasing

`Wanderer.Tick`, run over them all by `Wanderers.Tick`, is `WanderTick` (`0x8000f6a4` in 1.0). In 1.0, choosing the next node is `0x8000f4c8`, setting off `0x8000f5b4` and `WanderGetNextNode` `0x8000f3e0`, the port's `Wanderer.Choose`, `Resume` and `Take`. Enemies notice a walking Alaron about 13 in front and 7 behind, he can sneak up behind them, and they chase him to their leash and stand there idle.

- **When.** Each frame in trek, unless the screen is fading, the game is paused, any widget is up (a popup, a conversation, the menu: `WHANDLE->GetTail()`), or the game state isn't trek's. The port's trek calls it each tick after the player moves, unless a popup or a conversation is up or a teleport is fading, and the menu stops trek altogether. It doesn't care how far a wanderer is from the camera, though one beyond 30 doesn't move (above).
- **The timer** counts down by the frame's ticks every frame, whatever the wanderer is doing; the port counts 1 a tick. It's a u16 the code compares as signed (`lh`, `bgtz`), so it moves on at 0 or below and goes on counting down.
- **Perception** is the party's entity's (`EntityDB::GetPerception`, 10 or 0), and 10 for a dialogue entity. Some talkers' entities are plain ones with 0; talkers never use it. It's measured from a point 0.3 × perception ahead of the body to the player, across all three axes: *perceived*. The plain distance is used for talkers and the ambush.
- **The ranges** are set once, in doubles, when perceived ≤ 2 × perception and they aren't set: p = perception × (1 − `fogTime` × 0.75), with `fogTime` the weather's (`Weather.FogTime`); walking p, running 1.5p, the most 2p, sneaking 0.6p × s, anything else 0.55p × s, where s = `sneak_value(0.8)` = 1 − min(the best Stealth, modified / 10, 1) × 0.8, or 0 with no party (`NoticeRanges`). The party's Stealth counts as it was when they were set: they stay until they're cleared, below.
- **Giving up and ending a chase** come first. Let *both out* be the body and the player both at or beyond the party's radius (the leash) from the party's position, across the ground. If the wanderer is chasing and either perceived ≥ the most or both out, or if the ranges are set and perceived ≥ 2 × perception (chasing or not, talkers too):
  - both out, it stops where it stands (`ResetMoveQueue`), its timer becomes 300 and its node flag 4 is set (`0x8000fa38`–`fa54`), so when the timer runs out it goes back to its goal rather than choosing another;
  - otherwise, if chasing, it goes back to its goal at once (`Wanderer.Resume`);
  - either way the ranges are cleared, and the chase ends if perceived ≥ the most or the body is at least the leash + 2 from home. So a body just past its leash after a player still in range stops, and the chase sets it off again the same tick, until it's 2 past. There it stands for 300 ticks; as it turns for home, the point it measures from swings round, and if the player is still near enough that the ranges were set again, he's now 2 × perception or more from it, so it gives up again where it stands, and again, until he goes further off.
  - The code measures the player's distance from the party's position in the party's chunk's coordinates against the player's in his own (it converts the position, then uses the unconverted one), so for a wanderer in another chunk that is wrong. The port measures in the world's coordinates.
- **Noticing.** A hunter (`moveType` 1: a party without a dialogue) notices the player when its body is within the leash of home across the ground, the wanderer's flag 1 isn't set (`Calmed`: set by `setFlag1`, cleared by `Wanderer.Resume`), and perceived ≤ the range for the player's request: 3 running, 2 walking, 25 sneaking, and the standing range for anything else. Each comparison includes its bound. The code also notes for the battle what the player was doing (`EncounterDat.aniByte` 3, 2, 1 or 0), which picks both sides' spawn set ([combat](combat.md#the-arenas)); the port takes it as the wanderer catches him (`TrekLoop.AniByte`).
  - In the original, one hunter noticing makes every wanderer after it in the list chase that frame: the flag is cleared once before the loop (`0x8000f71c`; set at `0x8000fb20`–`fb74`). So while one enemy sees Alaron, every enemy listed after it anywhere in the 3×3 runs at him, whatever its leash, and can ambush him, and talkers after it stop moving on. The port has each notice for itself, as the user chose (ADR 0018).
- **Talkers** (`moveType` 0) within 3 of the player (strict, across all three axes) are asked for request 0 and turn to face him (`SetFacing`, which forgets their goal), every frame. While turning they don't move on, so a talker stays facing him while he's near; when he leaves it waits out its timer.
- **Moving on.** Not noticed and not chasing: when the body isn't moving, turning or held (`playerData` flags & 0x70) and the timer is 0 or below, it goes back to its goal with flag 4 (clearing it), or else chooses the next node (`Wanderer.Choose`), and sets off (`Wanderer.Resume`). Talkers wander so too.
  - **The next node:** a roll of `GetFloat0ToX(1)` under the current node's chance takes its second next node, otherwise the first; there's no check on either. `WanderGetNextNode` copies the new node's values in, its wait among them, so **the wait starts when the node is chosen**, and a long walk uses it up. If the new node is one to walk to, its scatter follows: an angle `GetFloat0ToX(2π)` (`0x40c90fdc`) and a length `GetFloat0ToX(scatter)`, in that order, the goal moved by (sin, cos) × length in X and Z. The first node, as the body appears, has none. Some nodes name themselves as both next nodes (I08's 0x0FB6 node 211), so the wanderer picks a new spot in the same node each time.
  - **Setting off** (`Wanderer.Resume`, `0x8000f5b4`) clears flag 1; walks or runs to the goal if it's beyond the node's radius, by the party's flag 0x2 (above); and at a node to turn to, turns to face the node: it's given the way from the node to the body (`Vec2Sub`), which `SetFacing` takes as the original's reversed facing, so it faces the node, not away.
- **Chasing.** Noticed or still chasing, it's chasing, and a hunter runs at the player, within 2 (`SetAiDest` with walk 0, `0x8000fc0c`; walk sets the body's walk-only flag), again every frame.
  - **The ambush:** within 7 of him (strict, `0x8000fc28`), with `enemyHostileFlag` (1, and nothing in 1.0 clears it) and no dialogue: if |Δy| < 3 and a sphere of radius 0.5 swept from the body's centre + 1 to the player's + 1 hits nothing in the wanderer's chunk (`CheckCollision`), the battle starts as an ambush (`collisionByte` 2; [combat](combat.md#in-and-out)) and it stops hunting (`moveType` 0). The port sweeps the whole loaded world.
- **Flee** (`moveType` 2, running directly away to within 1) is in the code, but nothing sets it.
- **Random numbers** come from the wandering stream. `GetFloat0ToX` (`0x80093428`) is 0 without a draw for 0, and otherwise `GetFloat0To1` (`0x800933d4`) × X in single precision: the new seed as an unsigned number, rounded to a float, × 2⁻³² (`0x2f800000`), which is X itself for the top 128 values.

## Talking

- **What A reaches** (`MapObjectUse.Reach`, `0x80004654`): the player's nearest body joins the map objects A weighs, first in the list, if it passes (`0x80004480`): it's a wanderer's (`findWandererFromPlayerName`; the nearest body only, so a wanderer just behind another body isn't reached), the push loop's distance across the ground to it (the body's `+0x108`, a tick old) is at most its scaled radius plus the player's plus 0.5 (`0x80004520`–`453c`), they're less than 2 apart in height (`0x800cd134`), and its party isn't silent (flag 0x4), hunters' included. Alone it's used; among others it's scored with that distance and its body's position, and loses a tie as the first. Facing counts only in the score.
- **Using a hunter** starts a battle: `collisionByte` 0 with the player in a cone ahead of its body and 1 otherwise (which only turns the enemies' starting facing; the port always passes 0, [combat](combat.md#not-settled)), `aniByte` 0, the party's flag B as the battle's flag. Trek fades out into it ([combat](combat.md#the-port)).
- **Using a talker** (`IMapObjectHost.Talk`, `0x800109fc`; nothing for a silent party): its script starts (`Script.Start`, reference point 0, the party's index for what the script remembers), then the wanderer is held: calmed (flag 1), its body held (flag 0x40) and in the conversation (flag 0x800), both asked for request 0, and each turns to face the other (`SetFacing`); then the party's flag B is set. Nothing stops a body still walking; a talker within 3 of the player has already stopped to face him (above). The game stays in trek (game state 3), with the box as a widget.
- **During it**, the world runs on: bodies move and animate, the sky and chunk streaming go on, but the clock, `WanderTick` and the HUD stop, and the player gets no input. Every frame (`0x80051bd8`) the wanderer is held again and both are asked for request 0.
- **The camera** frames the two (`Bodies.Process`, `CreateFocusFromMultiple`): see [cameras](cameras.md#the-conversation-camera).
- **Talking.** Each button press (`0x80051c4c`) asks both for request 0, moves the conversation on, and then, unless the script played an animation in that press (`ApplyStimulus`), asks the speaker for request 4: Alaron if the shown line's speaker is Alaron, and otherwise the wanderer, whoever the speaker is (`0x800514cc`). A line with no speaker entity animates nobody. The next frame's request 0 replaces it.
- **The end** (`0x80051ccc`), from the script's end, a shop, training, a battle or a camp: a shop, training or battle takes the game out of trek (a script's battle uses the party's flag C, not B). The camera goes back to following the player (`GiveCameraToPlayer`, the pitch back to its default) with a quick swing of 5 frames. The wanderer's body is let go and it sets off for its node again (`Wanderer.Resume`), which ends its calm; or, if a member joined in the conversation (`GameState.AddMember`, only in a conversation), its body is freed, and what keeps it from coming back when its chunk next loads is its party's enabled gate (see [joining](#joining)). Map objects aren't gated again; only a chunk loading does that, or a cut-scene's end, which loads them all again (see [joining](#joining)).
- **Dialogue objects** (`IMapObjectHost.StartScript`, `0x80010d68`) start their script with their own reference point and index, and hold no body. Of the 364 in the base plugin, 271 are cut-scenes, 72 backdrops and 20 signs; the one conversation is a placeholder ("this dialog isn't BORGED yet", map 0x127B, object 2). A sign shows the box with the player asked for request 0, and nothing else changes. A cut-scene fades out and leaves trek ([scripts](scripts.md#cut-scenes)); a backdrop leaves it at once (`TrekLoop.ShowBackdrop`). One already running stops another starting.

### In the port

`Conversation` (`AidynEnhanced.Core.Trek`) is a script running in trek, with a wanderer (`Conversation.TalkTo`) or a sign (`Conversation.Show`), on the world random stream. `TrekLoop` keeps the push loop's nearest body for A (`Wanderers.Reaches`), and its `IMapObjectHost.Talk` starts a talker's conversation or a hunter's battle. Trek loads a chunk's wanderers as its zone engine loads the chunk, before the chunk's sounds and lights. Where it's not plain from the code:

- **The player turns to the talker as a body does,** 10° a frame where he stands (`PlayerMotion.Face`).
- **Walking into objects and onto triangles stops** during a conversation, except for their cameras, which take the camera from the conversation's framing.
- **The talk request lasts a tick:** turning, or a steered body's step, overrides it, as `Actor::Move` does in the original.
- **A line that plays an animation** (`PlayAnimation`) has its speaker's body, chosen as for the talk, asked for the request in place of the talk as it shows, for a tick as well, if the speaker's actor has a state for it; if not, the speaker talks. The line shows once. That's the port's fix: in 1.0 the speaker talks, nobody plays the request, and the line shows again until A ([scripts](scripts.md#ops-that-need-more-than-a-flag)).
- **A conversation's scripts don't move the camera or walk anyone:** what one would wait for, but a timer, is taken as done (`TrekLoop.Refresh`).
- **The end** (`Conversation.End`) leads where the script's ending says ([scripts](scripts.md#ops-that-need-more-than-a-flag)): a shop, training, a camp's hours or a battle.

### Joining

A member's joining or leaving is in [characters](characters.md#joining-and-leaving).

- **The speaker's body is freed** at the end (`0x80051ccc`, `Actor::FreePlayer`) when a conversation's AddMember worked (`GameState.AddMember`, `0x80050970`, sets `dialogmode+0xA8` when the script's start function is 0). A cut-scene's AddMember doesn't set it.
- **A cut-scene rebuilds trek instead.** `0x80051F4C`, which ends every cut-scene, backdrop and help script, sets their done flag and `+0xA8` too, but nothing reads that outside a conversation's end. Trek frees its world, wanderers and all (`TrekLoop.Free`, `0x800211a4`), as a cut-scene starts, and builds it again at its end (`initGameTrek`, `0x80020f40`; [scripts](scripts.md#the-world-it-plays-in)), gating every map object again and starting every wanderer whose party is still there at its home node. So Godric's late-game cut-scene (`0x0529`) takes him off the map because his party is there only while flag 90 is clear. Of the 45 monster parties whose script is a cut-scene, 42 are gated off by what their script sets; the other three (`0x042F`'s two and `0x050B`'s) come back at their home nodes. The port rebuilds trek the same way (`Conversation.RebuildsTrek`, `TrekLoop.Rebuild`).
- **What keeps a recruit away is their party's enabled gate.** Nearly every recruit's monster party is enabled by a derived flag that holds NOT their joined flag. For example:
  - Keelin's in Terminor (map `0x1436`, party 17) by 5413 = NOT 265 & NOT 619 & 363 & 263.
  - Godric's (`0x18D0`, party 4) by 5391, which also reads 73, the flag his script sets.

  So a member who leaves comes back to where they were recruited, which is how the ones dismissed come back in Terminor.
- **The exceptions.**
  - Niesen's and Farris's recruiting dialogue objects are gated by their scripts' done flags (373, 413 and 449).
  - Sholeh's party in Ugarit (`0x118A`, party 1, for `aidyn:dialogue/0x04AD`) is enabled by 5432 = 490 & NOT 622 & NOT 492, which nothing her joining sets. By the data she'd be there again, and her script, now greeting her return (491), runs AddMember again: the original would add a second Sholeh ([characters](characters.md#joining-and-leaving)); the port doesn't, but frees her body again. The user remembers her not appearing at all without a free place, so something else may keep her away ([user-checks](../user-checks.md)).
- **Five joining scripts nothing starts:** `0x03AA` (Godric), `0x045B` (Becan), `0x045C` (Abrecan), `0x045D` (Godric) and `0x04EC` (Trahern). No map object or script names them.
